        BAGM: Learning Spatial Cell Representations in Gastric Tissue

                                                 Abstract

Spatial transcriptomics enables cell states to be studied within their tissue context. We introduce Bio-Architectural Graph Modeling
(BAGM), which learns cell representations by reconstructing randomly masked RNA expression using the remaining molecular
measurements and spatial neighborhoods. Relative geometry modulates graph attention, and training minimizes Huber loss at masked
entries. In gastric tissue, Leiden clustering of the learned representations showed clearer qualitative correspondence with clinician-
identified cancer regions than PCA, with improved delineation after four attention layers. However, expression-prediction precision,
recall, and AUPRC remained limited. These observations motivate quantitative validation, evaluation on larger datasets, and
investigation of learned dependencies through attention and sensitivity analyses.
